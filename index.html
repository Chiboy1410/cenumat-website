CENUMAT · Faculty of Veterinary Medicine · University of Ibadan

We map how the brain
is built, fed, and broken.

CENUMAT studies neuroanatomy, developmental neuroscience and environmental neurotoxicology across species — from Wistar rats to African giant rats, squirrels and bats.

Twenty-five years tracing how metals, nutrition and disease reshape the nervous system, from the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway to whole-brain metal distribution.

A member laboratory of the Society of Neuroscientists of Africa, training researchers through IBRO/ISN schools and Humboldt fellowships.

Explore our research Meet the lab

Founded within the Department of Veterinary Anatomy as CENUMAT - the Centre of Neuroscience with Novel Models and Alternative Therapies - the group investigates neurodegeneration, developmental neuroscience, and the neurotoxic impact of environmental exposures such as vanadium and heavy metals, alongside the protective roles of nutrition and phytochemicals. Work spans rodents, squirrels, bats and other indigenous species, putting comparative anatomy at the centre of translational neuroscience in Africa.

Research Focus

Four questions guide the bench.

01

Environmental Neurotoxicology

Chronic exposure to vanadium and heavy metals — brain metal distribution, demyelination, and neuroinflammation across generations.
02

Neuroprotection & Phytotherapy

Plant-derived and synthetic compounds tested against oxidative and toxicant-induced injury via Nrf2/HO-1, NF-κB and Bax/Bcl-2/Caspase-3 pathways.
03

Comparative & Developmental Neuroanatomy

Histology and immunohistochemistry of the visual, olfactory and limbic systems in squirrels, bats, owls and African giant rats.
04

Neuroendocrine & Reproductive Toxicology

Hormonal and testicular consequences of toxicant exposure, bridging neuroscience with theriogenology.
