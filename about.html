Our Story

Comparative anatomy at the centre of translational neuroscience in Africa.

Founded within the Department of Veterinary Anatomy as CENUMAT — the Centre for Neuroscience with Novel Models and Alternative Therapies — the group investigates neurodegeneration, developmental neuroscience, and the neurotoxic impact of environmental exposures such as vanadium and heavy metals - alongside protective roles of nutrition and phytochemicals. Work spans rodents, squirrels, bats, and other indigenous species, situating comparative anatomy at the centre of translational neuroscience in Africa.

The group is a member laboratory of the Society of Neuroscientists of Africa (SONA) network and has trained researchers through IBRO/ISN schools, Humboldt fellowships, and postgraduate supervision across theriogenology, neuroanatomy and toxicology.

Principal Investigator

Prof. James Olukayode Olopade

DVM, PhD, FCVSN, FAS FNSN FAAS - Professor of Neuroscience & Comparative Anatomy

DVM, University of Ibadan, 1992. Alexander von Humboldt Fellow at the University of Würzburg (2010–2011) and IBRO Research Fellow at Pennsylvania State University (2008). Head of the Department of Veterinary Anatomy (2014–2017), Dean of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine (2017), Director of Veterinary Teaching Hospitaal University of Ibadan (2021-2024) and President of the Society of Neuroscientists of Africa (elected 2017). Research spans neurovirology, neuroprotection, developmental neuroscience and vanadium neurotoxicity.
